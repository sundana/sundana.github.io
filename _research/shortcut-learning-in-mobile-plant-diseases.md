---
title: "When the Background Becomes the Diagnostic: Shortcut Learning in Mobile Plant Diseases Classifiers"
status: ongoing
desc: Diagnosing why imagined rollouts destabilize lightweight MBRL. Controlled encoder-matched experiments show multi-step latent drift corrupts returns; horizon truncation to h=1 restores stability.
---

Smartphone application for diagnosting crop diseases mostly rely on lightweight convolutinal neural networks that have been validated only on laboratory images. This study examined whether such models remain reliable in the field. Seventeen lightweight models, including MobileNetV3 variants with squeeze-and-excitation (SE), convolutional block attention module (CBAM) or no attention, were trained on PlantVillage images of ten crops and tested without adaptaion on 1783 field images of 20 matched classes from PlantDoc. All models reached 96.9-99.4% accuracy on PlantVillage, but only 0-8.1% in the field, and the best field F1-score was 10.9%. Between 23 and 37% of field images were assigned to the PlantVillage "background without leaves" class, and activation maps showed attention to soil and shadows rather than to symptomps, revealing a background shortcut. Diseases with large, conspicuous lesions, such as tomato late blight (28,8%), were recognised best, whereas healthy leaves were almost never recognised (3.0%). At equal reduction ratios, SE and CBAM differed by less than 0.04% in parameters and neither improved field accuracy over the model without attention. Fine-tuning on field images raised accuracy to 34-40%, whereas CutMix/MixUp augmentation did not help (0.0%). Laboratory accuracy therefore does not predict field performance. Based on these findings, we propose a mobile diagnostic framework that combines leaf detection and segmentation, a quantised on-device classifier, confidence-based abstention with expert referral, and fine-tuning on regional field images.
